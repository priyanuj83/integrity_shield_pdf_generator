\item \textbf{Answer:} Kinesis and taxis are two distinct forms of animal behavior that describe how organisms respond to stimuli in their environment. Kinesis refers to a non-directional movement where an animal alters its activity level or speed in response to a stimulus, but does not orient its body toward it; for example, woodlice increase their movement in dry conditions to find moisture. In contrast, taxis involves a directional movement where an organism moves toward or away from a specific stimulus, such as moths flying toward a light source at night. These behaviors illustrate different strategies animals use to navigate their environments, with kinesis providing a more random response and taxis offering a targeted approach. Understanding these concepts is crucial in studying how animals adapt and survive in various ecological contexts.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it accurately distinguishes between kinesis as a non-directional response characterized by changes in activity level and taxis as a directional movement toward or away from a stimulus, while providing relevant examples that illustrate each concept in natural settings.

\item \textbf{Answer:} Swallowing involves a coordinated sequence of physiological mechanisms to ensure that food and air are directed into their respective pathways. When food or liquid is ingested, pressure receptors in the pharynx are stimulated, triggering a reflex that elevates the larynx. This elevation causes the epiglottis to fold down over the glottis, effectively sealing off the airway and preventing aspiration. As a result, the bolus of food is channeled into the esophagus, which is situated behind the trachea. This intricate interplay between the pharynx, larynx, and epiglottis is essential for safe swallowing, allowing simultaneous respiratory and digestive functions to occur without interference.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it accurately describes the reflexive actions of the pharynx, larynx, and epiglottis during swallowing, highlighting how the elevation of the larynx and the closure of the epiglottis prevent food from entering the airway and ensure proper routing into the esophagus.

\item \textbf{Answer:} Recombinant DNA is formed through various methods, primarily including restriction enzyme digestion, ligation, and transformation. Restriction enzymes cut DNA at specific sequences, allowing scientists to isolate desired genes, while ligation involves joining the DNA fragments using DNA ligase to create a stable recombinant molecule. Transformation refers to the introduction of this recombinant DNA into host cells, where it can be replicated and expressed. In contrast, translation is a biological process that synthesizes proteins from mRNA templates but does not involve the manipulation or joining of DNA molecules. Thus, translation does not qualify as a method for forming recombinant DNA, as it occurs after the DNA has already been modified and does not contribute to the creation of recombinant DNA itself.

\item \textbf{Answer:} The movement of electrons between orbitals in an atom is primarily influenced by the principles of quantum mechanics, particularly the quantization of energy levels. Electrons can transition between these orbitals when energy is absorbed or released, allowing them to occupy different regions around the nucleus. Despite these movements, the energy of the electrons remains constant when they shift between orbitals of the same energy level, a concept known as degeneracy. This is because the electron's position is described by probability distributions rather than fixed paths, allowing for changes in location without variation in energy. Such transitions are crucial in biological processes, such as photosynthesis and cellular respiration, where energy transfer is essential.
\\ \textit{Note:} The answer correctly identifies that electron movement between orbitals is governed by quantum mechanics and energy quantization, emphasizing that transitions can occur without energy change when moving between degenerate orbitals of the same energy level.

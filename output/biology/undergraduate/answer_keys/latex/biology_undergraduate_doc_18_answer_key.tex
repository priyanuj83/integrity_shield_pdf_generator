\item \textbf{Answer:} The transmission of impulses in plants is evidenced by the physiological responses of the sensitive plant, Mimosa pudica, to external stimuli. When the leaves of Mimosa pudica are touched, they exhibit rapid folding and drooping, a response that occurs within seconds. This movement is facilitated by changes in turgor pressure within the plant cells, which are triggered by electrical signals that propagate through the plant's vascular system. This phenomenon demonstrates that plants are capable of rapid signaling similar to nerve impulses in animals, indicating a form of communication and response to environmental factors. Overall, the quick reactions of Mimosa pudica to touch provide compelling evidence for the existence of impulse transmission in plants.

\item \textbf{Answer:} Fermentation plays a critical role in cellular respiration under anaerobic conditions, where oxygen is scarce. During this process, glucose is partially broken down to produce ATP, but more importantly, fermentation regenerates NAD+ from NADH. This regeneration is essential because NAD+ is required for glycolysis, the initial step of cellular respiration that produces a small yield of ATP. Without the replenishment of NAD+, glycolysis would halt, leading to a complete cessation of energy production. Therefore, while fermentation yields less energy than aerobic respiration, it allows cells to continue producing ATP and survive in low-oxygen environments.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it accurately describes how fermentation allows for the regeneration of NAD+ from NADH, which is crucial for sustaining glycolysis and thereby enabling continued ATP production in the absence of oxygen.

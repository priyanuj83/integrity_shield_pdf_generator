Incomplete dominance occurs when two alleles of a single gene interact to produce a phenotype that is a blend of the traits associated with each allele, exemplified by the pink flowers resulting from the cross of red and white flowering plants. In contrast, epistasis involves the interaction between two different genes, where the presence of one gene's alleles can mask or modify the expression of another gene's alleles, leading to variations in phenotypic outcomes. While both concepts demonstrate complex genetic interactions, they differ fundamentally in that incomplete dominance focuses on the interaction within a single gene, whereas epistasis emphasizes the relationship between multiple genes. Understanding these concepts enhances our comprehension of genetic variation and inheritance patterns.

In economics, this concept is significant as it allows economists to make informed decisions and predictions about economic behavior, policies, and trends by ensuring a high level of confidence in their estimates. When conducting repeated sampling, it is expected that 99\% of the constructed confidence intervals will contain the true value of the null hypothesis, thereby providing a solid basis for statistical inference. This increased demand for good Z leads to a rise in its equilibrium price, as suppliers respond to the heightened demand. However, if the price of good X increases significantly, it may lead to a decrease in the equilibrium quantity of good Z if consumers begin to substitute more towards other alternatives or if the overall market demand contracts. In this context, the equilibrium price of good Z will rise while the equilibrium quantity could either increase or remain stable, depending on the elasticity of demand and the extent of the price change for good X.

\item \textbf{Answer:} The use of money in trade presents several advantages over barter, primarily by facilitating transactions and expanding trading opportunities. Unlike barter, which requires a double coincidence of wants-where both parties must have what the other desires-money eliminates this restriction, allowing individuals to trade more freely. Money serves as a medium of exchange, a unit of account, and a store of value, which streamlines the trading process by providing a common measure for valuing goods and services. Consequently, this broadens the scope of trade, enabling individuals to engage in transactions with a wider range of partners without the limitations imposed by barter. In essence, money enhances economic efficiency and allows for more complex economic interactions, fostering greater market participation and economic growth.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it highlights how money, as a medium of exchange, overcomes the limitations of barter by eliminating the need for a double coincidence of wants and providing a standardized measure for valuing goods, thereby facilitating transactions and broadening trading opportunities.
\item \textbf{Answer:} In the income-expenditure flow, 'withdrawals' refer to any use of income that does not contribute directly to consumption expenditure on the current output of the domestic economy. Examples of withdrawals include savings, taxes, and imports, which reduce the overall demand for domestically produced goods and services. Conversely, 'injections' are expenditures that stimulate economic activity but do not arise directly from consumer income-expenditure decisions, such as government spending, investment, and exports. These injections counterbalance the effects of withdrawals by increasing overall demand and promoting economic growth. The interaction between withdrawals and injections is crucial, as a disparity between the two can lead to economic fluctuations, influencing levels of employment, production, and overall economic stability.
\\ \textit{Note:} The answer correctly identifies withdrawals as factors that decrease domestic demand, such as savings, taxes, and imports, while defining injections as external expenditures, like government spending and investments, that stimulate economic activity, thereby illustrating their contrasting roles in the income-expenditure flow and their overall impact on the economy.
\end{enumerate}

\item What happens to the purchasing power of money when there is inflation? What does it mean?

\item The market for denim jeans is in equilibrium, and the price of polyester pants, a substitute good, rises. In the jean market
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item supply falls, increasing the price and increasing the quantity.
    \item demand rises, decreasing the price and increasing the quantity.
    \item demand rises, increasing the price and decreasing the quantity.
    \item demand falls, decreasing the price and increasing the quantity.
    \item supply rises, decreasing the price and increasing the quantity.
\end{enumerate}
\item A production possibility frontier will be a straight line when
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item efficiency is achieved
    \item there is no opportunity cost
    \item utility is maximized
    \item resources are not specialized
    \item there is economic growth
\end{enumerate}
\item The "crowding-out" effect refers to which of the following?
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item Higher interest rates that result from borrowing to conduct contractionary fiscal policy
    \item Lower interest rates that result from borrowing to conduct expansionary monetary policy
    \item Lower interest rates due to borrowing to conduct contractionary fiscal policy
    \item Lower interest rates resulting from reduced borrowing due to expansionary fiscal policy
    \item Lower interest rates due to increased borrowing to conduct expansionary monetary policy
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

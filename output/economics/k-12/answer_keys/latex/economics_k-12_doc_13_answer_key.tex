\item \textbf{Answer:} Rising price levels can significantly affect the effectiveness of government expansionary fiscal policy and the resulting spending multiplier. When the government increases spending or cuts taxes to stimulate the economy, the idea is that this will lead to more consumer spending and investment. However, if prices are rising, consumers may be less likely to spend additional money because their purchasing power decreases, which can dampen the intended effects of the fiscal policy. Additionally, high inflation can lead to increased interest rates, making borrowing more expensive and reducing the overall impact of the government's efforts. Consequently, the spending multiplier, which measures how much economic activity is generated from initial spending, may be weaker in an environment of rising prices.

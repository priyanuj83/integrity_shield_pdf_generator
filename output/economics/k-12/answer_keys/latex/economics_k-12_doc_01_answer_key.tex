\item \textbf{Answer:} An increase in demand for the goods produced by labor leads to a rise in the equilibrium market wage in a competitive labor market. When consumers want more of a product, companies need to produce more, which requires hiring additional workers. This heightened demand for labor drives up wages as employers compete to attract the necessary workforce. As wages increase, it can also encourage more people to enter the labor market, further supporting the production of the desired goods. Ultimately, the stronger demand for these products translates into higher wages for workers, reflecting their increased value in the production process.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it outlines the relationship between increased demand for goods and the consequent rise in labor demand, which leads to higher wages as employers compete for workers to meet production needs.
\item \textbf{Answer:} Transfer earnings in economics refer to the minimum amount of income a worker could earn in their next best alternative job, which reflects their opportunity cost. For example, if a cashier at a restaurant earns \$8 an hour, this wage is likely similar to what other cashiers earn in a competitive job market. In this scenario, the cashier's transfer earnings are equal to their actual earnings because they could earn the same amount elsewhere, indicating that their wage is determined by the market rate for cashiers. This concept highlights that the cashier's income does not exceed what they could earn in other comparable positions, emphasizing the balance between labor supply and demand in determining wages.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it accurately defines transfer earnings as the minimum income a worker could earn in an alternative job, illustrates this with the example of a cashier whose earnings reflect market conditions, and demonstrates how these earnings are determined by competition in the job market.

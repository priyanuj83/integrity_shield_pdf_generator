\item \textbf{Answer:} A price ceiling is a government-imposed limit on how high a price can be charged for a product or service. When a price ceiling is set below the equilibrium price, it leads to a situation where the quantity demanded exceeds the quantity supplied, resulting in a shortage. This occurs because consumers are willing to buy more at the lower price, but producers are not willing to supply enough to meet that demand. The implications of this are that some consumers may be unable to purchase the product, leading to inefficiencies in the market. Overall, while price ceilings aim to make goods more affordable, they can disrupt the balance between supply and demand.
\\ \textit{Note:} The answer correctly identifies a price ceiling as a government limit on prices, explains that setting it below the equilibrium price creates a shortage by increasing quantity demanded while decreasing quantity supplied, and highlights the resulting implications for consumer access to the product.
\item \textbf{Answer:} An increase in the money supply in the economy typically leads to a decrease in nominal interest rates. When more money is available, banks have more to lend, which increases the supply of loans and decreases the cost of borrowing money, reflected in lower interest rates. As nominal interest rates fall, it becomes cheaper for consumers and businesses to borrow, encouraging spending and investment. This can stimulate economic growth as more money circulates through the economy, leading to increased consumption and potentially higher levels of employment. However, if the money supply grows too quickly, it could also risk inflation, which can offset some of these positive effects.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because an increase in the money supply leads to lower nominal interest rates, which reduces the cost of borrowing, thereby encouraging spending and investment that stimulates economic growth.

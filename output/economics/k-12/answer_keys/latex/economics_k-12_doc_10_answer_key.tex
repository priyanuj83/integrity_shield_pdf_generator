\item \textbf{Answer:} Import quotas are government-imposed limits on the quantity of a specific good that can be imported into a country. One predictable economic consequence of import quotas is that they lead to higher consumer prices, as the reduced supply of imported goods often results in less competition, allowing domestic producers to raise their prices. Additionally, import quotas can cause a misallocation of resources among producers, as they may protect less efficient domestic industries while limiting the ability of more efficient producers to compete. This can lead to an overall decrease in economic efficiency, as resources are not being utilized in the most productive manner. Ultimately, while import quotas may shield certain domestic industries, they negatively impact consumers and the economy as a whole.

This happens because higher interest rates offer better returns on investments in that currency, attracting foreign investors. As demand for the currency increases, its value appreciates, meaning it becomes stronger compared to other currencies. However, a stronger currency can negatively impact net exports, as it makes a nation's goods more expensive for foreign buyers, leading to a potential decrease in exports. For example, if the U.S. raises its interest rates, the dollar may strengthen, making American products pricier abroad and possibly reducing demand for exports.

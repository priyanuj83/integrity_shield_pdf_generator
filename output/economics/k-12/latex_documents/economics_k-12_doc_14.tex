\section*{Multiple Choice (10 marks)}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item GDP is calculated for each by
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item quarter; The Bureau of Economic Analysis
    \item week; The Bureau of Economic Analysis
    \item month; The Bureau of Economic Analysis
    \item month; The Bureau of Labor Statistics
\end{enumerate}
\item Opportunity costs or implicit costs of a "Mom \& Pop"-owned business are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item equal to accounting costs.
    \item equal to accounting profits.
    \item equal to earnings or profits that could have occurred using resources elsewhere.
    \item equal to earnings or profits that occurred for Mom \& Pop's business.
\end{enumerate}
\item When the price of pears increases, we expect the following:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item Quantity demanded of pears rises.
    \item Quantity supplied of pears falls.
    \item Quantity demanded of pears falls.
    \item Demand for pears falls.
\end{enumerate}
\item When the value of the U.S. dollar appreciates relative to other currencies which of the following is the most likely to occur?
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item Imports into the United States will decrease.
    \item Exports from the United States will increase.
    \item U.S. residents will take more vacations in foreign countries.
    \item More foreign visitors will travel to the United States.
\end{enumerate}
\item When a firm is earning a normal profit from the production of a good, it is true that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item total revenues from production are equal to explicit costs.
    \item explicit costs are equal to implicit costs.
    \item total revenues from production are equal to implicit costs.
    \item total revenues from production are equal to the sum of explicit and implicit costs.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

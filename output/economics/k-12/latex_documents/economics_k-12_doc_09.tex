\section*{Multiple Choice (10 marks)}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item In an oligopoly market, firms
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item cannot earn economic profits
    \item are interdependent
    \item are not subject to antitrust legislation
    \item are large in number
\end{enumerate}
\item If the demand for grapes increases simultaneously with an increase in the supply of grapes, we can say that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item equilibrium quantity rises, but the price change is ambiguous.
    \item equilibrium quantity falls, but the price change is ambiguous.
    \item equilibrium quantity rises, and the price rises.
    \item equilibrium quantity falls, and the price falls.
\end{enumerate}
\item Suppose you observe an economy where prices are falling and real GDP is rising. This may have been caused by
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item stagflation.
    \item an advance in technology.
    \item an increase in government spending.
    \item a decrease in government spending.
\end{enumerate}
\item Which of the following is a predictable advantage of expansionary monetary policy in a recession?
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item Decreases aggregate demand so that the price level falls.
    \item Increases aggregate demand which increases real GDP and increases employment.
    \item Increases unemployment but low prices negate this effect.
    \item It keeps interest rates high which attracts foreign investment.
\end{enumerate}
\item Stagflation is the result of
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item a leftward shift in the aggregate supply curve.
    \item a leftward shift in the aggregate demand curve.
    \item a leftward shift in both the aggregate supply and aggregate demand curves.
    \item a rightward shift in the aggregate supply curve.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

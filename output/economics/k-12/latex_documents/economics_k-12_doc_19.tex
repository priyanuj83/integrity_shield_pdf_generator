\section*{Multiple Choice (10 marks)}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item Suppose that an expansionary fiscal policy leads to a large increase in real output and a small increase in the price level. From this it can be inferred that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item inflation had already impacted the economy before the fiscal stimulus.
    \item the economy initially had some unemployed resources.
    \item aggregate supply decreased.
    \item aggregate demand is steeply sloped.
\end{enumerate}
\item Suppose a nation is experiencing an annual budget surplus and uses some of this surplus to pay down part of the national debt. One potential side effect of this policy would be
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item increase interest rates and throw the economy into a recession.
    \item increase interest rates and depreciate the nation's currency.
    \item decrease interest rates and risk an inflationary period.
    \item decrease interest rates and throw the economy into a recession.
\end{enumerate}
\item Consumer surplus is
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item the price of a good divided by its marginal utility.
    \item the marginal utility of the good divided by its price.
    \item the total utility of the good.
    \item the difference between the consumer's value and the market price.
\end{enumerate}
\item A bilateral monopoly exists when
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item a monopsony buys from a monopoly
    \item a monopoly sells to two different types of consumers
    \item a monopoly buys from a monopsony
    \item a monopolist sells two different types of goods
\end{enumerate}
\item Households demand more money as an asset when
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item nominal GDP falls.
    \item the nominal interest rate falls.
    \item bond prices fall.
    \item the supply of money falls.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Answer:} The fundamental differences between the short-run and long-run Phillips curves lie in their shapes and the implications for inflation and unemployment within economic policy. The short-run Phillips curve is downward sloping, indicating an inverse relationship between inflation and unemployment; as inflation rises, unemployment tends to decrease, primarily due to nominal wage rigidity and price stickiness. Conversely, the long-run Phillips curve is vertical, suggesting that in the long term, there is no trade-off between inflation and unemployment; the economy tends to gravitate towards the natural rate of unemployment regardless of the inflation rate. This distinction implies that while policymakers can exploit the short-run trade-off through expansionary policies to reduce unemployment, such measures may lead to accelerating inflation without permanently lowering unemployment in the long run. Therefore, effective economic policy must consider these differing dynamics to avoid the pitfalls of inflationary spirals.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it accurately describes the short-run Phillips curve's downward slope indicating a trade-off between inflation and unemployment due to nominal wage rigidity, while also correctly identifying the long-run Phillips curve's vertical position, which implies that this trade-off does not persist in the long term, thus influencing economic policy decisions.

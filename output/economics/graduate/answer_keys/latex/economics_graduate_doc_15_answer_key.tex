\item \textbf{Answer:} Capital goods and consumer durables exhibit characteristics that make them particularly vulnerable during a recession, primarily due to their postponability and the implications of market power. The postponability of these goods means that consumers and businesses can delay purchases when economic conditions are unfavorable, leading to a sharp decline in demand. This behavior is exacerbated by market power dynamics; firms with monopoly power may maintain higher prices, deterring potential buyers further during downturns. Consequently, the combination of reduced consumer confidence and the ability to defer purchases results in a significant contraction in sales for both capital goods and consumer durables, amplifying the recession's impact on these sectors. Thus, understanding these characteristics is crucial for mitigating risks associated with economic downturns.

\item \textbf{Answer:} Differences in inflation rates between the European Union (EU) and the United States (U.S.) significantly influence the exchange rate between the euro and the U.S. dollar. When inflation is high in the EU while the price level in the U.S. remains stable, the purchasing power of the euro declines relative to the dollar. This decline leads to an appreciation of the dollar against the euro, as investors and consumers seek to hold assets in a currency that retains its value. Additionally, higher inflation in the EU may prompt the European Central Bank to adjust interest rates, further impacting capital flows and exchange rates. Thus, the interplay between inflation differentials and monetary policy responses is crucial in determining the strength of the dollar relative to the euro.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it illustrates how relatively higher inflation in the EU reduces the euro's purchasing power, prompting investors to prefer the more stable U.S. dollar, thereby driving its appreciation against the euro.

This situation highlights broader economic implications, such as the need for workforce retraining and adaptability in response to technological changes, as well as the potential for increased inequality if displaced workers cannot transition to new roles. \item \textbf{Answer:} In the context of aggregate demand, imports are subtracted because they represent spending on foreign goods, which stimulates production in other countries rather than domestically. This outflow of spending not only reduces the overall demand for domestic goods but can also negatively impact domestic employment as local industries may see a decline in sales and, consequently, job opportunities. Conversely, exports are added to aggregate demand as they signify domestic goods sold to foreign markets, thereby increasing domestic production and potentially creating jobs within the local economy. By promoting exports, a nation can enhance its economic growth, balance its trade, and support local employment, illustrating the importance of these components in calculating aggregate demand.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it accurately reflects the economic principle that imports reduce domestic aggregate demand and employment by directing consumer spending away from local production, while exports enhance aggregate demand and support domestic employment by increasing demand for domestic goods in foreign markets.

As the government withdraws fiscal stimulus, the demand for borrowing typically outpaces the supply, prompting lenders to raise interest rates to attract savings. Higher interest rates tend to strengthen the dollar in the short term as foreign investors seek higher returns on U.S. assets. However, over time, the contractionary measures can lead to reduced economic growth, which may decrease investor confidence and result in a depreciation of the dollar. This complex interaction illustrates that while initial effects can bolster the dollar, sustainable contractionary policies may ultimately weaken it as economic fundamentals shift.

\item \textbf{Answer:} Pure public goods are characterized by two primary features: non-excludability and non- rivalrous consumption. This means that once a public good is provided, it is impossible to prevent individuals from using it, and one person's use does not diminish its availability for others. A key implication of these characteristics is that the marginal cost of providing an additional unit of the good to additional citizens is zero; this allows for an unlimited number of individuals to benefit from the good without incurring further costs. Consequently, this leads to challenges in funding and maintaining public goods, as private markets may underprovide them due to the inability to charge consumers directly. Therefore, government intervention is often necessary to ensure the adequate provision of public goods, reflecting their essential role in promoting societal welfare.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it accurately identifies the defining traits of pure public goods-non-excludability and non-rivalrous consumption-which directly lead to the conclusion that the marginal cost of providing additional consumption to citizens is zero, thereby allowing for widespread access without diminishing the good's availability.

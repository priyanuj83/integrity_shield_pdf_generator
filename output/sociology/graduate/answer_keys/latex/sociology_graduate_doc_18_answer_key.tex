\item \textbf{Answer:} The concept of the social construction of reality, as articulated by Berger and Luckmann, posits that individuals actively negotiate shared definitions of their experiences and social situations. This process is inherently dynamic, as individuals interpret and re- interpret their interactions within a social context, thus creating a collective understanding that shapes their reality. However, as these definitions become internalized, individuals often forget that their social worlds are not fixed or objectively determined; instead, they are fluid constructs that can change over time. The implications of this phenomenon are profound, as it suggests that societal norms and values are not merely external forces but are continuously shaped and reshaped through individual and collective actions. This understanding encourages critical reflection on the taken-for-granted aspects of social life, highlighting the potential for change and the role of agency in the ongoing construction of reality.

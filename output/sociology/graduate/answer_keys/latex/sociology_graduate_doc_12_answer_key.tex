\item \textbf{Answer:} Marx's assertion that religion would diminish following a socialist revolution is rooted in the belief that such a revolution would eradicate the capitalist ideology that underpins religious belief systems. In a capitalist society, religion often serves to justify social hierarchies and economic inequalities, providing a sense of hope or justification for the oppressed. By dismantling the structures of capitalism, a socialist revolution would remove the socio-economic conditions that enable religion to thrive as a tool of alienation and oppression. Consequently, as the material conditions of life improve and equality is established, the need for religious consolation diminishes, leading to a decline in religious belief and practice. This transformation reflects Marx's view that ideology, including religion, is shaped by the material realities of society, and thus, a fundamental change in those realities would precipitate a corresponding shift in ideological frameworks.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it articulates Marx's view that the socio-economic conditions of capitalism perpetuate religious beliefs that justify inequality, and that a socialist revolution would eliminate these conditions, leading to a decline in religious influence.
\item \textbf{Answer:} Totalitarian societies are characterized by an absolute concentration of power in a central authority, which seeks to control not only the political and economic spheres but also the social and cultural dimensions of life. A defining feature of such regimes is the significant restriction of citizen freedoms, particularly the freedom of movement. In totalitarian systems, the state often imposes severe limitations on where individuals can travel, both domestically and internationally, as a means to monitor, control, and restrict dissent. This suppression of mobility reinforces the regime's dominance by preventing the spread of alternative ideas and fostering a climate of fear and conformity. Ultimately, the curtailment of movement exemplifies the broader nature of totalitarianism, where individual autonomy is systematically dismantled in favor of collective obedience to the state.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it identifies the concentration of power and the systematic restriction of individual freedoms, particularly freedom of movement, as fundamental characteristics that define the oppressive nature of totalitarian societies.

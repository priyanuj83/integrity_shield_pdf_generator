\item \textbf{Answer:} Globalization has significantly altered the power dynamics of nation-states by challenging traditional state authority in various ways. One major implication is the rise of transnational corporations and international organizations, which often hold economic and political power that can rival or even surpass that of sovereign states. Additionally, the flow of information and ideas across borders diminishes the control that states have over their populations, as citizens are increasingly influenced by global cultures and ideologies. Furthermore, issues such as climate change and terrorism require cooperative international responses, which can undermine the unilateral decision-making power of nation-states. Thus, while globalization extends the power of nation-states in some areas, it simultaneously complicates their authority and autonomy in the face of interconnected global challenges.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it identifies the emergence of transnational corporations and international organizations as powerful entities that undermine state authority, alongside the global dissemination of information that empowers citizens and reduces state control.
\item \textbf{Answer:} Wallerstein's concept of a 'world system' situates the capitalist world economy as a complex, interconnected structure characterized by a global division of labor that transcends ethnic and cultural boundaries. Within this system, various regions and groups contribute to the production and consumption processes in distinct ways, often based on historical and socio-economic contexts. The significance of this division of labor lies in its role in perpetuating inequalities and reinforcing power dynamics, as certain ethnic and cultural groups may be relegated to peripheral roles while others dominate core economic activities. Consequently, the capitalist world system not only reflects but also shapes the interactions among diverse cultural groups, illustrating how economic practices are deeply embedded in social relations and cultural identities. This framework allows sociologists to analyze the implications of globalization and the uneven development that arises from these interconnected labor divisions.
\\ \textit{Note:} This answer is correct because it accurately captures Wallerstein's framework of a world system by highlighting the interconnected nature of the capitalist economy and the critical role that a global division of labor plays in shaping socio-economic inequalities among diverse ethnic and cultural groups.

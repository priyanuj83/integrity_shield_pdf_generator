\item \textbf{Answer:} Auguste Comte's contribution to sociology is profoundly significant as he is credited with coining the term "sociology" and establishing it as a distinct discipline in the 19 th century. His work marked a pivotal shift in social thought, moving away from speculative philosophy towards a scientific approach to studying society. Comte proposed a systematic methodology for examining social phenomena, emphasizing the importance of empirical observation and the use of scientific methods, which laid the groundwork for future sociological research. This endeavor reflected the broader intellectual climate of the time, characterized by an increasing reliance on reason and science to understand human behavior and social structures. Consequently, Comte's ideas not only formalized the study of society but also influenced subsequent thinkers, establishing a framework that continues to inform contemporary sociology.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it accurately identifies Auguste Comte as the founder of sociology, highlights his introduction of a scientific methodology for the study of society, and contextualizes his contributions within the broader shift in 19 th-century social thought from philosophical speculation to empirical and systematic analysis.
\item \textbf{Answer:} In the nineteenth century, naturalistic explanations of race were predominantly influenced by biological determinism and the emerging fields of anthropology and ethnology. Factors such as skin color, cranial measurements, and hair texture were emphasized as indicators of racial superiority or inferiority, aligning with the prevailing ideologies of colonialism and social Darwinism. Height, however, was often overlooked as a significant racial marker, possibly due to its less distinct correlation with social hierarchies and the complexities of environmental influences on physical development. This neglect reflects a tendency to prioritize traits that could be simplified into rigid classifications, thereby reinforcing existing power dynamics rather than acknowledging the multifaceted nature of human variation. By focusing on easily observable characteristics, scholars of the time failed to engage with the broader social and environmental factors that shape human diversity.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it accurately identifies the emphasis on biological determinism and specific physical traits in the racial discourse of the nineteenth century, while also noting the overshadowing of height as a less relevant factor in the context of prevailing colonial and social Darwinist ideologies.

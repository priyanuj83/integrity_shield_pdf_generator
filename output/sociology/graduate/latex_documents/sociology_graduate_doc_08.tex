\section*{Multiple Choice (10 marks)}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item War became possible between nation states in the nineteenth century because:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item the world was divided into several rival overseas empires
    \item industrialization provided better transport, technology and administration
    \item there was intense economic, political and military competition
    \item all of the above
\end{enumerate}
\item Which of the following was not defined by Giddens (1998) as part of New Labour's 'third way':
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item the democratization of the family
    \item putting an end to privatization
    \item positive welfare: 'a hand up, not a hand-out'
    \item the strengthening of civil society
\end{enumerate}
\item Dahrendorf, Rex, and Habermas focused their attention on:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item social solidarity and cohesion
    \item the interpretive understanding of action
    \item women's experiences and gendered knowledge
    \item power, domination, and conflict
\end{enumerate}
\item The media-themes model of media influence claims that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item audiences selectively interpret what they want to hear
    \item content analysis is the best way to identify the themes covered by the media
    \item audiences passively absorb whatever messages they are given
    \item social interaction reinforces the ideas and images that audiences select
\end{enumerate}
\item The 'class polarization' that Marx predicted referred to:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item the division of each social class into the more or less privileged
    \item a growing gap between rich and poor, resulting in class consciousness
    \item the growth of intermediate strata in the middle classes
    \item the tendency for the working class to live in very cold places
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

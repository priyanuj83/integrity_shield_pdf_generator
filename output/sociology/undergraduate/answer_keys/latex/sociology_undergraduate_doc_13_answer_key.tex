\item \textbf{Answer:} The concept of 'research purpose' in sociology refers to the specific aim or objective that drives a sociological investigation, guiding the formulation of research questions, selection of methodologies, and interpretation of findings. Research purposes can range from exploratory to explanatory, each serving distinct functions in understanding social phenomena. Triangulation, which involves using multiple methods or data sources to validate findings, does not align with this definition because it is primarily a methodological strategy rather than a purpose in itself. While triangulation can enhance the robustness of research, it does not inherently define the aim of the research; instead, it serves to strengthen the conclusions drawn from a given research purpose. Thus, while both concepts are vital in sociological research, their roles and definitions are fundamentally different.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it accurately defines 'research purpose' as the guiding aim of a sociological study, while contrasting it with triangulation, which is a methodological approach rather than a research aim.
\item \textbf{Answer:} Totalitarian societies are characterized by centralized control, a single-party rule, pervasive propaganda, and the suppression of dissent. In such regimes, the lack of freedom of movement significantly impacts citizens' lives by restricting their ability to travel, access information, and connect with others outside their immediate environment. This confinement fosters a climate of fear and obedience, as individuals are unable to seek alternative perspectives or challenge the status quo. Additionally, the societal structure becomes rigid, with a populace that is often disengaged and reliant on the state for information and direction, thereby perpetuating the regime's power and control. Ultimately, the absence of mobility not only stifles personal freedom but also undermines social cohesion and the potential for collective resistance.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it accurately identifies the defining features of totalitarian societies and explains how restricted movement contributes to a culture of fear and obedience, ultimately limiting citizens' access to diverse perspectives and reinforcing the regime's control.

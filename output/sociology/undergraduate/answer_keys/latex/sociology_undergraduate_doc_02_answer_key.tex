\item \textbf{Answer:} Modernization theory in sociology posits that societies progress through a series of stages from traditional to modern industrial forms of organization, driven by technological advancement, economic development, and social change. It suggests that traditional societies, characterized by subsistence agriculture and communal living, evolve into modern societies marked by industrialization, urbanization, and individualism. This transition is facilitated by the adoption of new technologies and the shift from agrarian economies to industrial and service-oriented economies. Proponents argue that such changes lead to improved living standards, greater education, and enhanced political participation. However, critics of modernization theory highlight its Eurocentric bias and neglect of the complexities and variations in development across different cultures.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it accurately describes modernization theory as a framework that outlines the progression of societies from traditional to modern industrial structures, emphasizing the roles of technological advancements, economic growth, and shifts in social organization.
\item \textbf{Answer:} Marx's assertion that religion would cease to exist following a socialist revolution is rooted in his belief that capitalist ideology creates a framework in which religious belief flourishes as a means of coping with social and economic inequalities. In a capitalist society, religion often serves to justify the status quo and provide solace to the oppressed, effectively masking the harsh realities of exploitation. However, with the implementation of a socialist revolution, the fundamental structures that perpetuate capitalist ideology-such as class divisions and material deprivation-would be dismantled. As these conditions are alleviated, the societal need for religion as an opiate to soothe suffering would diminish, leading to its eventual decline. Thus, Marx posits that the transformation of economic relations would inherently reduce reliance on religious beliefs, resulting in a more secular society aligned with socialist principles.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it articulates Marx's view that religion is a product of capitalist ideology that serves to rationalize inequality, and that a socialist revolution would eliminate the conditions that give rise to religious belief by addressing the underlying social injustices.
\end{enumerate}

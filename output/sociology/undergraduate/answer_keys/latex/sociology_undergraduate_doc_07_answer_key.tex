\item \textbf{Answer:} Townsend's (1979) indicators of relative deprivation focus on the inability to participate in activities and consume goods that are considered normal or necessary within a specific societal context. In this framework, relative deprivation is not solely about income levels but also about the social consequences of lacking certain material goods, which can lead to feelings of exclusion. The consumption of fewer than twenty DVDs in the previous year may not align with Townsend's indicators because owning or consuming DVDs is not universally seen as a necessity for participation in social life today. Instead, the rise of digital streaming services has shifted the normative expectations around media consumption, suggesting that fewer DVD purchases may not indicate deprivation but rather a change in consumption patterns. Thus, individuals may not experience relative deprivation despite having fewer physical DVDs, as their access to similar or alternative forms of entertainment remains intact.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it emphasizes that Townsend's indicators of relative deprivation assess social exclusion resulting from the inability to access goods and experiences deemed normal in society, which is not adequately reflected by the mere consumption of fewer than twenty DVDs in a year.
\item \textbf{Answer:} Official statistics on strike action in sociology often fail to capture the full scope of labor unrest, particularly due to the prevalence of unnoticed strikes by both employers and the mass media. Many minor or spontaneous strikes occur outside formal reporting mechanisms, leading to underrepresentation in official data. Employers may dismiss smaller actions as insignificant, while the media often prioritizes larger, more dramatic strikes, neglecting those that are less visible. This gap in reporting can skew public perception and academic understanding of labor dynamics, as the true extent and nature of worker dissent remain obscured. Consequently, reliance on official statistics can perpetuate an incomplete narrative about labor relations and the effectiveness of strikes in advocating for workers' rights.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it identifies that official statistics on strike action are incomplete due to the exclusion of minor or spontaneous strikes, which are often overlooked by employers and the media, leading to a significant underrepresentation of labor unrest in sociological research.

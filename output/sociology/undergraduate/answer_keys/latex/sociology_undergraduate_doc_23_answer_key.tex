\item \textbf{Answer:} Differential association theory posits that individual behavior is influenced by the norms and behaviors of the groups to which one belongs, particularly peers. This can be illustrated by the analogy of friends jumping off a bridge; if all of your friends decided to jump off, it is likely that you would feel compelled to follow suit due to social pressure and the desire for acceptance. This theory suggests that behaviors, whether positive or negative, are learned through interactions and associations with others. Consequently, individuals are more inclined to engage in actions that are normalized within their peer group, illustrating the powerful impact of social influences on personal choices. Thus, the choices we make, including risky or deviant behaviors, are often reflections of the groups we associate with.
\\ \textit{Note:} The answer correctly illustrates differential association theory by demonstrating how peer influence and the desire for social acceptance can lead individuals to adopt behaviors, such as jumping off a bridge, that they may not engage in independently.
\item \textbf{Answer:} The functionalist perspective in sociology views society as a complex system composed of interdependent and coordinated parts, much like the organs of a body working together to maintain homeostasis. Each social institution, such as family, education, and government, plays a crucial role in contributing to the overall stability and functioning of society. This interdependence implies that changes in one part of the system can impact others, potentially leading to social stability or disruption. For instance, when education systems adapt to new economic needs, they can help maintain social order by preparing individuals for the workforce. However, too much change too quickly can lead to dysfunction, highlighting the delicate balance required for social cohesion.
\\ \textit{Note:} The answer correctly illustrates the functionalist perspective by emphasizing the interdependence of social institutions and their collective role in maintaining societal stability, while also acknowledging that changes within one part of the system can influence the whole, thus affecting both stability and social change.
\end{enumerate}

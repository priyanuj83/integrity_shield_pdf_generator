\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]

    \item A secondary group
    \item An aggregate
    \item A social category

\item Murray thought that the 'underclass' consisted of people who:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item formed an inferior 'race' with low levels of intelligence
    \item lived morally unsound lives of crime and squalor

\end{enumerate}
\item The effect of the Internet upon the public sphere has been to:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item repress it, by promoting only the interests of elite groups
    \item revive it, by reaffirming a commitment to freedom of speech
    \item reproduce it, by emphasizing face-to-face contact with peer groups

\end{enumerate}
\item Scott (1991) introduced the term 'power elite' to describe:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item the ruling class, or bourgeoisie, who exploit the proletariat
    \item a capitalist class dependent on property ownership and advantaged life chances
    \item an alignment of classes with shared interests but no state power
    \item a state elite whose members are drawn overwhelmingly from a power bloc
\end{enumerate}
\item Secularization involves the two related ideas of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]

    \item disbelief and disintegration

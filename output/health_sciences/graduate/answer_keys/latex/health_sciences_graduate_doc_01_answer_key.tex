\item \textbf{Answer:} Polyomaviruses, a family of small, double-stranded DNA viruses, play a complex role in human health, primarily due to their ubiquitous presence and frequent asymptomatic infections in the general population. While certain polyomaviruses, such as JC virus and BK virus, have been linked to diseases like progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) and renal complications in immunocompromised individuals, they are often considered non-pathogenic in healthy individuals. This non-pathogenicity can be attributed to the robust immune response elicited by the host, which effectively controls viral replication and prevents disease manifestation. As a result, most infections remain latent without causing any clinical symptoms, underscoring the importance of immune competence in mitigating the potential pathogenic effects of these viruses. Understanding this dynamic is crucial for public health, as it highlights the need for vigilance in at-risk populations while recognizing the generally benign nature of polyomavirus infections in healthy individuals.

\item \textbf{Answer:} The diagnostic criteria for osteopenia and osteoporosis in North America and Western Europe primarily hinge on bone mineral density (BMD) measurements obtained through dual- energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA). According to the World Health Organization (WHO), osteopenia is defined as a BMD that is 1 to 2.5 standard deviations below the mean for a young adult reference population, while osteoporosis is defined as a BMD that is 2.5 standard deviations or more below this mean. Age and gender significantly influence these assessments, as postmenopausal women, particularly those over age 80, often exhibit a decline in BMD due to hormonal changes. Interestingly, it has been observed that women in this age group may have higher-than-expected BMD for their age, which can complicate the diagnosis of osteopenia and osteoporosis, suggesting the need for careful interpretation of BMD data in the context of age-related bone health.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it accurately describes the WHO's definitions of osteopenia and osteoporosis based on specific BMD measurements, while also emphasizing the importance of age and gender as critical factors influencing these assessments.

HDL is known as "good" cholesterol because it helps remove cholesterol from the bloodstream, so lower levels can lead to increased plaque buildup in arteries. Small dense LDL particles are particularly harmful as they can more easily penetrate the arterial walls and contribute to atherosclerosis, a condition that narrows and hardens the arteries. While iron deficiency can lead to anemia and fatigue, high iron stores pose even greater risks in this population.

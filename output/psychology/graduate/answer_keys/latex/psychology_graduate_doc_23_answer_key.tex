\item \textbf{Answer:} Proactive and retroactive interference are key concepts that illustrate the complexities of information classification and retrieval in cognitive psychology. Proactive interference occurs when previously learned information disrupts the acquisition and recall of new information, making it difficult to learn or remember new material. Conversely, retroactive interference happens when newly learned information interferes with the retrieval of older memories, often leading to the forgetting of past knowledge. These phenomena underscore the dynamic nature of memory, highlighting how the interplay between old and new information can complicate cognitive processes. Understanding these interferences is crucial for developing effective strategies for memory enhancement and retrieval, as they reveal the intricate mechanisms by which information is organized and accessed within the mind.

\item \textbf{Answer:} Incremental Theory and One-trial Theory present contrasting views on learning processes in cognitive and motor tasks. Incremental Theory is predominantly supported in cognitive tasks, where knowledge and skills are acquired gradually through practice and experience, emphasizing that intelligence and abilities can develop over time. Conversely, One-trial Theory is more applicable to motor tasks, where learning can occur effectively after a single exposure or practice session, as seen in skills like riding a bike or throwing a ball. These theories have significant implications for educational practices; educators might focus on fostering continuous learning and resilience in cognitive areas, while emphasizing immediate practice and feedback in motor skill development. Understanding these distinctions can help tailor teaching strategies to optimize learning outcomes in different domains.

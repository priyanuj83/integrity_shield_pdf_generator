\section*{Long Form Response}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]

\item \textbf{Answer:} Neal Miller proposed that an organism in a conflict situation has several behavioral alternatives that depend on the proximity to a goal and the relative strengths of approach and avoidance drives. When an organism is close to a desired goal, the approach drive typically dominates, leading to behaviors that facilitate reaching that goal. Conversely, if the goal is perceived as being far away or if the avoidance drive is stronger-due to potential negative consequences-the organism may exhibit avoidance behaviors. Additionally, the intensity of these drives influences the likelihood of engaging in certain behaviors; a stronger approach drive in close proximity to the goal can result in persistence, while a stronger avoidance drive can lead to withdrawal or distraction. Thus, the interplay between goal proximity and drive strength crucially shapes the behavioral responses of an organism faced with conflicting motives.
\\ \textit{Note:} The answer correctly identifies that Neal Miller's theory posits that an organism's behavioral alternatives in conflict situations are influenced by the interplay of goal proximity and the relative strengths of approach and avoidance drives, leading to different behaviors based on these factors.
\item \textbf{Answer:} The projective hypothesis in psychology posits that an individual's social functioning can serve as a reflection of their underlying psychological processes. This relationship suggests that the way people interact with others and navigate social situations may reveal their internal thoughts, emotions, and unresolved conflicts. For example, individuals displaying aggression in social contexts might be projecting their own internal struggles or anxieties. Understanding this connection has significant implications, as it emphasizes the importance of psychological assessment in addressing social behavior issues, thereby aiding in therapeutic interventions and promoting healthier social interactions. Overall, the projective hypothesis highlights the intricate link between psychological functioning and social dynamics, reinforcing the idea that social behavior is not merely situational but deeply rooted in individual psychological states.
\\ \textit{Note:} The answer correctly identifies the projective hypothesis as a framework that connects an individual's social behavior to their internal psychological state, illustrating how external interactions can reveal internal conflicts and emotions.
\end{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Answer:} A Type I error occurs when a true null hypothesis is incorrectly rejected, leading researchers to conclude that there is a significant effect or relationship when, in reality, none exists. In psychological research, this can have serious implications, such as promoting ineffective interventions or therapies based on false positives. Additionally, it can mislead future research directions and waste resources, as subsequent studies may build upon erroneous findings. Ultimately, Type I errors undermine the credibility of psychological science and can lead to a loss of public trust in research outcomes. Thus, the consequences of rejecting a true null hypothesis extend beyond individual studies, impacting the broader field and its applications.

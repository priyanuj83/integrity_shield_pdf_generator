\item \textbf{Answer:} The relationship between customary law and treaty provisions is significant, as treaties can play a crucial role in shaping and recognizing customary law. Firstly, treaties may codify existing customary law by formally recognizing and articulating its principles, thereby providing a clear legal framework. Secondly, treaties can 'crystallise' customary law, which occurs when consistent state practice and a belief in legal obligation (opinio juris) are solidified through treaty commitments, enhancing their legitimacy. Lastly, treaties can lead to the emergence of new customary law by establishing norms that, over time, gain acceptance and become binding on states through consistent practice. Consequently, treaties serve not only as instruments of international law but also as catalysts for the evolution of customary law.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it highlights how treaties can both codify existing customary law and crystallize it by reinforcing state practice and the belief in legal obligation, thus influencing the development and recognition of customary law.
\item \textbf{Answer:} In international law, 'de facto' organs of the State refer to individuals or entities that, while not officially recognized as state organs, operate under the direction or control of the State and perform functions that are attributable to it. These entities may include private individuals, groups, or organizations that act on behalf of the State in a manner that reflects state policy or directives. The key implication of this concept is that the State can be held responsible for the actions of these de facto organs, as their conduct is considered an extension of the State's own actions. This attribution of responsibility underscores the importance of ensuring that all entities executing state functions adhere to international legal standards, thereby reinforcing accountability in state conduct. Ultimately, the recognition of de facto organs enhances the understanding of state responsibility in international law, particularly in situations where state authority may be exercised indirectly.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it accurately identifies 'de facto' organs of the State as non-official entities that act under state control and perform functions that can be attributed to the State, thus establishing grounds for state responsibility in international law.
\end{enumerate}

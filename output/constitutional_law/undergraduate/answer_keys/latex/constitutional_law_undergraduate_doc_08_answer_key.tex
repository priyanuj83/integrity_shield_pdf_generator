\item \textbf{Answer:} Hart's distinction between 'being obliged' and 'having an obligation' is crucial for understanding legal rules in constitutional law as it clarifies the nature of legal duties. 'Being obliged' refers to the external pressures or consequences that compel individuals to act in a certain way, often influenced by social or legal sanctions. In contrast, 'having an obligation' reflects a deeper, internalized understanding of a legal rule, where individuals recognize their duty based on established legal norms. This distinction helps us see that constitutional rules are not merely coercive mandates but are also grounded in a shared understanding of rights and responsibilities within a legal framework. Thus, it emphasizes the importance of both the normative and the practical dimensions of legal rules in shaping governance and citizen behavior.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it effectively differentiates between the external enforcement of legal duties ('being obliged') and the internal recognition of legal responsibilities ('having an obligation'), thereby enhancing our comprehension of how legal rules function and are perceived within constitutional law.
\item \textbf{Answer:} The revival of natural law in the 20 th century can be attributed to several interrelated factors, notably the recognition of international human rights following World War II. The atrocities committed during the war prompted a global reassessment of moral and legal standards, leading to the establishment of documents such as the Universal Declaration of Human Rights in 1948. This recognition emphasized inherent human dignity and rights that transcend national laws, aligning closely with natural law principles that advocate for universal moral norms. Additionally, the emergence of international human rights law provided a framework that reinforced the idea that certain rights are inalienable and rooted in human nature, thus revitalizing natural law discourse in legal and philosophical circles. Consequently, the post-war human rights movement not only reaffirmed the relevance of natural law but also integrated it into contemporary legal frameworks.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it identifies the post-World War II recognition of international human rights as a crucial factor in the revival of natural law, emphasizing the universal principles of inherent human dignity and rights that transcend national legal systems.

\item \textbf{Answer:} Ethical relativism posits that moral principles are not universal but are instead shaped by cultural contexts, which can lead to significant implications for human rights. This perspective may undermine the universality of human rights, as practices deemed acceptable in one culture could be viewed as violations of rights in another. In contrast, cognitivism argues that moral statements can be objectively true or false, which supports the notion of universal human rights that transcend cultural boundaries. By asserting that certain rights are inherent and not subject to cultural interpretation, cognitivism provides a robust counterargument to ethical relativism, reinforcing the foundation of constitutional law that seeks to protect individual rights irrespective of cultural differences. Thus, cognitivism advocates for a more consistent application of human rights, challenging the moral ambiguity posed by ethical relativism.
\\ \textit{Note:} The answer correctly identifies that ethical relativism challenges the universality of human rights by suggesting that cultural context determines moral values, while cognitivism counters this by asserting the objectivity of moral truths, thereby reinforcing the concept of universal human rights in constitutional law.

\item \textbf{Answer:} Cognitive models play a crucial role in understanding consumer behavior, particularly through the lens of the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB). This theory posits that an individual's intention to engage in a behavior is influenced by their attitudes, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control, thereby highlighting the cognitive processes behind decision-making. However, assuming rationality in decision-making can be problematic, as it overlooks the emotional and social factors that often drive consumer choices. This rationality assumption can lead to oversimplified models that fail to account for real-world complexities, ultimately limiting the effectiveness of marketing strategies. Understanding these dynamics is essential for businesses aiming to predict and influence consumer behavior more accurately.

\item \textbf{Answer:} Face validity refers to the extent to which a measure appears to assess what it is intended to measure, based on subjective judgment rather than statistical analysis. In data analysis, an analyst's critical perspective plays a significant role in evaluating data against expectations, as it shapes their interpretation of results and the perceived relevance of the data. A strong critical perspective allows analysts to identify potential biases and limitations in the data, thereby enhancing the credibility of their conclusions. Ultimately, while face validity is an important initial assessment, it must be complemented by rigorous statistical validation to ensure comprehensive and reliable data analysis.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it accurately defines face validity as a subjective assessment of whether a measure aligns with its intended purpose, while also emphasizing how an analyst's critical perspective can reveal biases and improve the interpretation of data in relation to established expectations.

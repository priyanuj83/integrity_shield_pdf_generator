For instance, through experiments or observational studies, companies can identify whether a reduction in price leads to increased demand in one location while not having the same effect in another. \item \textbf{Answer:} Grunig and Hunt's (1984) situational theory of publics identifies different types of publics based on their awareness and involvement in issues, classifying them into segments such as active, aware, and latent publics. Active publics are engaged and take action, while aware publics recognize issues but do not engage, and latent publics are unaware of the issues. Dormant publics, on the other hand, are excluded from this framework as they remain inactive and uninformed about any issues, thus lacking the potential for engagement or influence. This exclusion emphasizes the theory's focus on publics that can actively participate in communication processes, highlighting the importance of awareness and engagement in effective public relations strategies. Consequently, the situational theory prioritizes those groups that can contribute to dialogue and change, leaving dormant publics outside the scope of strategic communication planning.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it accurately describes Grunig and Hunt's classification of publics based on their awareness and engagement levels, emphasizing that dormant publics are excluded due to their complete inactivity and lack of awareness regarding any issues.

\item \textbf{Answer:} Civil society organizations (CSOs) can employ three contrasting tactics to achieve their objectives: indirect action, violent direct action, and non-violent direct action, such as boycotts. Indirect action often involves lobbying and advocacy efforts aimed at influencing policy through established political channels, which can foster sustainable change but may be slow and depend on the willingness of policymakers. In contrast, violent direct action, while potentially drawing immediate attention to issues, often leads to backlash and can undermine the legitimacy of the cause, alienating potential supporters. Non-violent direct action, including boycotts, seeks to leverage economic pressure to effect change without resorting to violence, often garnering broader public support and maintaining moral high ground.

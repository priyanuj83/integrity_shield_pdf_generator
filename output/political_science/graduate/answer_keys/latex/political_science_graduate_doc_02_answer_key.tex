However, diplomacy remains distinct from these economic tools as it prioritizes negotiation, dialogue, and relationship-building over coercion or incentives. \item \textbf{Answer:} The Responsibility to Protect (R$_{2}$P) doctrine is founded on three key principles: the state's responsibility to protect its population from mass atrocities, the international community's obligation to assist states in fulfilling this responsibility, and the readiness to intervene when a state fails to protect its citizens. However, the principle of sovereignty is often perceived as a limitation to R$_{2}$P, as it traditionally emphasizes non-interference in a state's internal affairs. This tension arises because sovereign states may prioritize their autonomy over the imperative to protect human rights, leading to reluctance in allowing external intervention even when necessary. Consequently, while sovereignty is a foundational aspect of international law, its interpretation can hinder the effective implementation of R$_{2}$P, creating a dichotomy between state sovereignty and the protection of human rights.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it accurately outlines the foundational principles of the Responsibility to Protect doctrine while critically addressing the conflict between state sovereignty and the international community's obligation to intervene in cases of mass atrocities, thus illustrating why sovereignty is viewed as a limitation to R$_{2}$P.

\item \textbf{Answer:} The ideological foundations of the American belief in establishing an 'empire of liberty' after 1776 were rooted in Enlightenment principles, emphasizing individual rights, self- governance, and the spread of democracy. Motivated by a vision of expanding republican ideals, American leaders perceived it as their duty to promote liberty both domestically and abroad, often justifying territorial expansion and interventionist policies under the guise of uplifting oppressed peoples. This belief not only influenced policies such as Manifest Destiny, which sought to extend U.S. territory westward, but also shaped foreign relations, leading to interventions in Latin America and beyond, aimed at fostering democratic governance. The implications of this ideology reflected a dual commitment to national expansion and a paternalistic approach to international engagement, which frequently contradicted the very principles of liberty and self-determination it professed to champion. Ultimately, the 'empire of liberty' concept became a critical lens through which American policymakers viewed their role on the global stage, intertwining notions of liberty with imperial ambitions.
\\ \textit{Note:} The answer correctly identifies the Enlightenment principles as foundational to the American ideology of an 'empire of liberty,' illustrating how these ideals motivated both domestic policies and foreign interventions aimed at promoting democracy and individual rights.
\item \textbf{Answer:} Historical materialism, as developed by Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels, posits that the material conditions of a society-its economic and productive capabilities-are the primary drivers of historical development and social change. This framework emphasizes that human societies progress through various stages of economic organization, which in turn shape political structures and ideologies. The implications for political theory and practice are profound; historical materialism critiques the notion of ideas as the sole catalysts for change, instead highlighting the role of class struggle and economic conflict in shaping political realities. Thus, it urges political actors to focus on the material and economic bases of society to understand and effectuate meaningful social transformation. This perspective remains influential in contemporary political analysis, particularly in discussions of capitalism, class relations, and social justice movements.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it accurately identifies historical materialism as a framework that connects material conditions and economic systems to historical development and political structures, thereby highlighting its critical role in understanding societal change and informing political theory and practice.

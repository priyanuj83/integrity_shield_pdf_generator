\item \textbf{Answer:} To calculate the total radiation force exerted on a completely reflecting surface of 3 $m^2$ at the Earth's surface with a solar flux of 1,000 W/$m^2$, we can use the formula for radiation pressure. The radiation pressure P exerted by light is given by P = I/c, where I is the intensity (solar flux) and c is the speed of light (approximately 3 x $10^8$ m/s). For a perfectly reflecting surface, the force F can be calculated as F = P \ensuremath{\times} A, where A is the area. Thus, P = 1,000 W/$m^2$ / (3 x $10^8$ m/s) results in a pressure of approximately 3.33 x 10^-6 N/$m^2$. Multiplying this by the area of 3 $m^2$ gives a total force of around 2 x 10^-5 N. This illustrates the principles of radiation pressure and momentum transfer from light to surfaces.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it applies the principles of radiation pressure and the conservation of momentum for light, using the formula for radiation pressure to calculate the force exerted on a reflecting surface based on the solar flux and the area of the surface.
\item \textbf{Answer:} Doubling the frequency of laser light in a double-slit experiment affects the separation of bright fringes observed on a screen. The fringe separation, or fringe spacing, is determined by the equation \( y = \frac{\lambda L}{d} \), where \( y \) is the fringe separation, \( \lambda \) is the wavelength of the light, \( L \) is the distance to the screen, and \( d \) is the slit separation. When the frequency is doubled, the wavelength \( \lambda \) decreases, since the speed of light is constant (\( c = \lambda f \)). Consequently, with a constant slit separation of 0.5 micrometers, the new fringe separation will be halved. Therefore, the separation of the bright fringes will become 0.5 mm, illustrating the direct relationship between wavelength and fringe spacing in diffraction patterns.
\\ \textit{Note:} Doubling the frequency of the laser light reduces the wavelength, leading to a narrower fringe separation according to the relationship between wavelength, slit separation, and fringe spacing in the double-slit experiment.

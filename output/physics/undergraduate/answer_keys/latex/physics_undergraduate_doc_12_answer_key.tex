\item \textbf{Answer:} In the hydrogen atom, an electron in the n = 4, l = 1 state can transition to a variety of lower energy states, specifically those allowed by the selection rules of quantum mechanics. The primary selection rules dictate that the change in the principal quantum number (Δn) can be any integer, while the change in the azimuthal quantum number (Δl) must be \ensuremath{\pm} 1. Therefore, an electron in the n = 4, l = 1 state can transition to the n = 3, l = 0 state or the n = 3, l = 2 state. However, transitions to states such as n = 4, l = 1 or n = 2, l = 1 are not allowed, as they violate the selection rules regarding changes in l or do not result in a decrease in energy. Thus, the only reachable final state from n = 4, l = 1 is n = 3, l = 1, which adheres to these quantum mechanical principles.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it accurately applies the quantum mechanical selection rules, specifically that Δl must be ±1, allowing transitions from the n = 4, l = 1 state to the n = 3, l = 0 and n = 3, l = 2 states, while also recognizing that certain final states are not reachable due to these rules.
\item \textbf{Answer:} In a one-dimensional inelastic collision between a particle of mass 2 m and a stationary mass m, the initial kinetic energy is solely due to the moving mass, given by KE\_initial = 1/2 (2 $m)v^2$ = $mv^2$, where v is the initial velocity of the moving mass. After the collision, the two masses stick together and move with a common velocity, which can be found using conservation of momentum: 2 mv = (2 m + m)V, leading to V = (2/3)v. The final kinetic energy of the combined mass is KE\_final = 1/2 (3 m)(2/3 $v)^2$ = 2/3 $mv^2$. The fraction of kinetic energy lost during the collision is then calculated as (KE\_initial - KE\_final) / KE\_initial = ($mv^2$ - (2/$3)mv^2$) / $mv^2$ = 1/3. Thus, the collision results in a loss of one-third of the initial kinetic energy.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it correctly applies the principles of conservation of momentum and kinetic energy to determine the initial kinetic energy, the final velocity after the inelastic collision, and subsequently calculates the fraction of kinetic energy lost during the collision.

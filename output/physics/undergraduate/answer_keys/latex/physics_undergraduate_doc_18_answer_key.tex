\item \textbf{Answer:} De Broglie hypothesized a fundamental relationship between the linear momentum (p) of a free massive particle and its wavelength (λ) through the equation λ = h/p, where h is Planck's constant. This relationship implies that every particle exhibits wave-like behavior, with its wavelength inversely proportional to its momentum. Planck's constant serves as a crucial proportionality factor, linking the macroscopic world of classical mechanics with the microscopic realm of quantum mechanics. In essence, as the momentum of a particle increases, its wavelength decreases, highlighting the dual wave-particle nature of matter and the significance of h in quantifying this behavior. This groundbreaking insight laid the foundation for modern quantum theory, illustrating the intrinsic connection between physical properties that were once thought to be distinct.

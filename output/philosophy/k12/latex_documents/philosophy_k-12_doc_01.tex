\section*{Multiple Choice (20 marks)}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item Which of the following conditions would make a slippery-slope argument fallacious?
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item The envisioned results of the action or practice under consideration turn out not to be bad.
    \item The central idea of the argument-that the action or practice under consideration will lead us down a path to disaster-turns out not to be plausible.
    \item both A and B are correct; either condition would make the argument fallacious.
    \item neither A nor B is correct; neither condition would make the argument fallacious.
\end{enumerate}
\item Select the best translation into predicate logic. Some CEO is wealthier than everyone. (Cx: x is a CEO; Px: x is a person; Wxy: x is wealthier than y)
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item (∃x)[Cx • (∀y)(Py • Wxy)]
    \item (∃x)[Cx ⊃ (∀y)(Py ⊃ Wxy)]
    \item (∃x)[Cx • (∀y)(Py ⊃ Wxy)]
    \item (∀x)[Px ⊃ (∃y)(Cy ⊃ Wxy)]
\end{enumerate}
\item Marquis argues that abortion is morally wrong by
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item showing that a fetus is a person with full moral rights, including the right to life.
    \item appealing to the fact that a fetus is biologically human and arguing that it is presumptively morally wrong to kill biologically human beings.
    \item showing that a fetus is sentient and that it is morally wrong to harm and kill sentient creatures.
    \item none of the above
\end{enumerate}
\item Advocates of care ethics argue that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item care ethics is an important development of virtue ethics.
    \item that traditional moral theories have difficulty explaining the rightness or wrongness of attitudes.
    \item that the only important more concepts are care and compassion.
    \item that we should think about welfare and happiness in terms of sympathy.
\end{enumerate}
\item The view that moral considerations do not apply to war is known as
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item jus ad bellum.
    \item antiwar pacifism.
    \item just war theory.
    \item moral nihilism.
\end{enumerate}
\item The black-or-white fallacy is another name for
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]

    \item False dilemma
    \item Begging the question

\item Given the following major premise, which of the following minor premises could lead to a valid conclusion? "Either I'm going to do well on this test or I'm...
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]

    \item "I won't take the test"

\item Velleman worries that, by offering a patient the option of euthanasia,

    \item we harm the patient.

\item According to de Marneffe, what is the central difference between drug legalization and mere decriminalization?
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item Legalization allows for the manufacture and sale of drugs; mere decriminalization doesn't.
    \item Mere decriminalization allows for the manufacture and sale of drugs; legalization doesn't.
    \item Legalization is a federal issue, but decriminalization is a state issue.
    \item none of the above; de Marneffe believes legalization and decriminalization are equivalent.

\item Arguing that someone should do something because other people are doing it is a description of which of the following fallacies?

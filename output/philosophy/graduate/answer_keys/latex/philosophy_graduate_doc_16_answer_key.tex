\item \textbf{Answer:} In response to the criticism that utilitarianism is a doctrine worthy of swine, John Stuart Mill counters by distinguishing between higher and lower pleasures, asserting that not all pleasures are equal in quality. He argues that intellectual and moral pleasures, which he considers superior, provide a greater satisfaction than mere physical pleasures. Mill emphasizes that the cost of pursuing these higher pleasures often involves a greater degree of effort, education, and personal development, which ultimately enriches human experience. This differentiation between the qualities of pleasure demonstrates that utilitarianism, rather than promoting a hedonistic lifestyle, advocates for a nuanced understanding of well-being that values the depth and richness of human fulfillment over mere sensory gratification. Hence, Mill asserts that a life dedicated solely to lower pleasures is less desirable and less human than one that seeks higher, more meaningful experiences.
\\ \textit{Note:} This answer is correct because it accurately reflects Mill's argument that utilitarianism values qualitative differences in pleasures, asserting that higher intellectual and moral pleasures, despite their greater effort and cost, offer a more profound satisfaction than mere physical pleasures, thereby addressing the critique that utilitarianism reduces human experience to mere animalistic indulgence.

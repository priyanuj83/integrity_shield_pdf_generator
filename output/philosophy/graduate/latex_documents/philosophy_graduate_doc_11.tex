\section*{Multiple Choice (10 marks)}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item Hume claims that a person who is unaffected by images of human happiness or misery will also be indifferent to:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item art and beauty.
    \item love and hatred.
    \item virtue and vice.
    \item success and failure.
    \item truth and falsehood.
\end{enumerate}
\item When a claim is supported only by asserting that unidentified experts accept the claim the arguer has committed which of the following fallacies?
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item snob appeal
    \item appeal to popularity
    \item post hoc ergo propter hoc
    \item appeal to anonymous authority
    \item straw man
\end{enumerate}
\item On McGregor's view, our rights carve out
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item the boundaries of our moral obligations.
    \item the domain of our personal identity.
    \item all of the above.
    \item what we are free to do.
    \item the responsibilities we have towards others.
\end{enumerate}
\item When Euthyphro attempts to define the holy as prosecuting wrongdoers, Socrates objects that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item it is impossible for humans to judge what is holy.
    \item there are many other things that are also holy.
    \item the concept of 'wrongdoing' is too subjective to define holiness.
    \item only gods can decide who the wrongdoers are.
    \item Euthyphro is not a reliable authority on what is holy.
\end{enumerate}
\item One suggestion that Lukianoff and Haidt make to challenge vindictive protectiveness is

    \item to increase the number of safe spaces on campus.
    \item to mandate sensitivity training for all students.
    \item to increase funding for mental health services on campus.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

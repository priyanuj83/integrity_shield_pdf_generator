\section*{Multiple Choice (10 marks)}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item Nagel claims that prior to reflection, it is plausible that people cannot be morally assessed for:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item actions that affect only themselves.
    \item actions performed under duress.
    \item actions that do not violate anyone's rights.
    \item actions that benefit others.
    \item what is due to factors beyond their control.
\end{enumerate}
\item Which of the following fallacies happen if someone argues that you are a reasonable and intelligent person, so of course you understand his claim is true?
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item argument from authority
    \item ad hominem attack
    \item begging the question
    \item red herring
    \item significance
\end{enumerate}
\item Hume claims that a person who is unaffected by images of human happiness or misery will also be indifferent to:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item art and beauty.
    \item love and hatred.
    \item virtue and vice.
    \item success and failure.
    \item truth and falsehood.
\end{enumerate}
\item Which mystic believed that evil was a distortion that reveals divine love more clearly?
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item Thomas Aquinas
    \item Catherine of Siena
    \item Teresa of Avila
    \item Hildegard of Bingen
    \item John of the Cross
\end{enumerate}
\item Baxter claims that his "very general way" of stating what we should strive for environmentally assumes that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item we have unlimited resources to address environmental issues.
    \item human satisfaction is the only measure of environmental success.
    \item we can measure in some way the incremental units of human satisfaction.
    \item we can precisely predict the environmental impact of our actions.
    \item the environment is solely a human construct.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

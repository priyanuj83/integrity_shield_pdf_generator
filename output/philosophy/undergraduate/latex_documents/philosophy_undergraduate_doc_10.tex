\section*{Multiple Choice (10 marks)}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item An example of a morally impossible rule would be:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item lie whenever doing so is necessary to prevent a catastrophe.
    \item always do whatever brings about the greatest happiness.
    \item always assert what you don't think to be the case.
    \item all of the above.
\end{enumerate}
\item Where did people believe in the goddess known as Holy Mother of Mount Fairy Peach?
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item China
    \item Silla
    \item Goguryeo
    \item Joseon
\end{enumerate}
\item Kant claims that the moral law is given to each person by:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item one's own will.
    \item one's own emotions.
    \item God.
    \item Nature.
    \item the universe.
\end{enumerate}
\item According to Singer, international corporations that deal with corrupt dictators are akin to
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item accomplices in the dictator's crimes.
    \item people who knowingly buy stolen goods.
    \item thieves who steal from the poor.
    \item merchants who sell harmful products.
    \item war profiteers exploiting conflict.
\end{enumerate}
\item Feinberg claims that the best way to pursue happiness is to:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item strive for success.
    \item pursue knowledge.
    \item pursue happiness.
    \item focus on material wealth.
    \item none of the above.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

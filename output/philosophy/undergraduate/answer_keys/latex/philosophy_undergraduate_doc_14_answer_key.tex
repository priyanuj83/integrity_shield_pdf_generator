\item \textbf{Answer:} In her alternative version of the violinist case, Judith Thomson argues that while one may have a moral obligation to allow the violinist to use their kidneys for an hour, this obligation does not equate to the violinist having a right to do so. The distinction lies in the difference between moral duty and rights; one can acknowledge that helping another is a commendable act without conceding that the person has a claim to one's body. Thomson uses this scenario to illustrate that moral considerations do not automatically translate into entitlements. Therefore, while it may be virtuous to assist the violinist, this does not justify the imposition of a right over one's bodily autonomy. This nuanced understanding highlights the complexity of moral obligations versus rights, emphasizing that ethical responsibilities do not necessarily confer ownership or claims over one's body.

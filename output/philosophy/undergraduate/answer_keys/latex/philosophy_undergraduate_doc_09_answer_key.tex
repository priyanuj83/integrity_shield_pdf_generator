\item \textbf{Answer:} A valid disjunctive syllogism is characterized by its structure, which typically consists of a major premise that presents two mutually exclusive alternatives and a minor premise that addresses one of those alternatives. The role of the minor premise is crucial, as it must either accept one alternative or reject the other to facilitate a valid conclusion. For instance, if the major premise states "Either A or B is true," the minor premise must assert "A is not true," leading to the conclusion that "B must be true." This logical framework emphasizes the importance of the minor premise in determining the validity of the argument by effectively guiding the acceptance or rejection of the proposed alternatives. Thus, the interplay between the premises is essential for deriving sound conclusions in disjunctive syllogisms.

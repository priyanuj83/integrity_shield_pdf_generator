\section*{True / False (20 marks)}
\textit{Answer True or False and justify briefly.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item True or False: The correct answer to 'Polonius in William Shakespeare's Hamlet: "Your noble son is mad: Mad call I it, for to define true madness, What is't but to be nothing...' is 'Begging the Question'.
\item True or False: The correct answer to 'Hume argues that the criteria for judging aesthetic value are \_\_\_\_\_.' is 'unchangeable'.
\item True or False: The correct answer to 'A hasty generalization is a fallacy of...' is 'Acceptable but untrue premises'.
\item True or False: The correct answer to 'Mill claims that the principle of utility:' is 'can be proven from principles that are known empirically.'.
\item True or False: The correct answer to 'We must assume that he is a Communist, because he says that he is not, and we know that Communists are taught always to deny party...' is 'Denying the Antecedent'.
\item True or False: The correct answer to 'Baier argues that moral rules:' is 'must applied to everyone alike in the absence of morally relevant differences.'.
\item True or False: The correct answer to 'According to Singer, the conclusions argued for in "all animals are equal" flow from which of the following principles alone?' is 'the principle of minimizing suffering'.
\item True or False: The correct answer to 'Applying irrelevant standards to the subject of the argument is the fallacy of' is 'false criteria'.
\item True or False: The correct answer to 'The use of a word in two different senses in an argument is the fallacy of...' is 'Equivocation'.
\item True or False: The correct answer to 'When someone is aware of evidence that contradicts her claim, but doesn't consider that evidence when making her argument, she may have...' is 'suppressed evidence'.
\end{enumerate}

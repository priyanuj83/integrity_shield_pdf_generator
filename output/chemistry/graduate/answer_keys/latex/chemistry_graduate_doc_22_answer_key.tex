\item \textbf{Answer:} To determine the pH of a 0.2 M NH₃ solution, we start with the equilibrium reaction for the base ionization of ammonia: NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻. The base dissociation constant, K\_b, is given as 1.8 \ensuremath{\times} 10⁻⁵. Setting up the equilibrium expression, K\_b = [NH₄⁺][OH⁻]/[NH₃], and letting x represent the concentration of OH⁻ produced, we can express this as K\_b = (x)(x)/(0.2 - x). Assuming x is small relative to 0.2, we simplify to K\_b = $x^2$/0.2. Solving for x gives us x = \ensuremath{\sqrt}(K\_b \ensuremath{\times} 0.2) = \ensuremath{\sqrt}(1.8 \ensuremath{\times} 10⁻⁵ \ensuremath{\times} 0.2) \ensuremath{\approx} 0.00134 M for [OH⁻]. The pOH is then calculated as pOH = -log(0.00134) \ensuremath{\approx} 2.87, leading to a pH of 14 - pOH = 14 - 2.87 = 11.13. Thus, the pH of the 0.2 M NH₃ solution is approximately 11.13, confirming that the solution is basic and consistent with the expected behavior of ammonia.
\\ \textit{Note:} correct because it correctly identifies the base ionization reaction of ammonia, applies the appropriate equilibrium expression for K\_b, and utilizes the assumption of negligible x to simplify the calculation for the hydroxide ion concentration, ultimately allowing for the determination of pH from the resulting concentration of hydroxide ions.

Upon heating, the cuprous ions precipitate out of solution as copper(I) oxide (Cu₂O), which appears as a brick-red solid. The underlying chemical process involves the oxidation of the sugar and the concomitant reduction of copper ions, demonstrating a classic example of redox chemistry in biological analysis.
